Google Workspace, Microsoft 365 and Zoho Mail: How to Choose

Published November 2026 · 2 min read

A plain comparison of the three most common business email platforms.

Most organizations do not run their own mail server anymore. They rent business email from a provider that handles storage, spam filtering and security. The three names you will see most often are Google Workspace, Microsoft 365 and Zoho Mail. Each can work well, and the right choice depends on how your team already works.

Google Workspace gives you Gmail on your own domain together with Drive, Docs, Sheets and Meet. Teams that already use Google tools, work mostly in a browser and like real-time shared documents often prefer it.

Microsoft 365 provides Outlook email together with Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Teams and OneDrive. Organizations that depend on desktop Office files, or that use Windows computers managed by an IT team, usually find it the natural fit.

Zoho Mail is a smaller provider that offers business email and a set of office tools, and it is often used by small teams that want a simple setup. Check the current plans on the provider's website, since features and limits change.

When you compare them, look at five things: the monthly cost per user, how much storage each mailbox gets, how easy it is to add security features such as two-step verification, how well it works with the tools you already use, and how good the support is in your time zone.

Whatever you pick, set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC on your domain right away, turn on two-step verification for every account, and keep a backup administrator account. Those steps matter more for your security than the brand you choose.

Questions to ask before you decide

How many people will need mailboxes now, and how many in a year? Do you work mostly on shared documents, or on files that live on each person's computer? Who will manage the accounts, and how comfortable are they with the admin tools? Does your industry have rules about where email is stored?

Moving an existing mailbox

Moving from one provider to another usually means changing the domain's MX records and migrating old mail. Plan the change for a quiet time, lower the record's time to live a day beforehand, and keep the old service running until you confirm new mail arrives.

A word on testing

Most providers offer a free trial or a short test period. Try the actual daily tasks, such as sending large attachments, sharing a calendar and reading mail on a phone, before you commit your whole team.
