Why Workplace Humor Makes Teams Stronger

Published November 2026 · 2 min read

A little laughter at work can help people cope and connect.

Walk into almost any office, shop or workshop and you will find running jokes: the printer that only works when no one is in a hurry, the meeting that could have been a message, the coffee machine that has outlived three managers. These small jokes do more than fill time. Used well, they help teams work together.

Humor builds connection

Laughing together is one of the quickest ways for colleagues to bond. Shared jokes show that people have something in common and create a feeling of belonging. New team members often feel more welcome once they are let in on the running jokes.

It helps people cope with stress

Many jobs involve pressure, deadlines or repetitive tasks. A joke about a shared frustration makes the problem feel smaller and reminds people they are not alone. This is why nurses, drivers, engineers and support staff are known for their dark and dry humor.

It makes hard conversations easier

A light remark can open a discussion that would otherwise feel awkward, such as admitting that a process is not working. When people can laugh at a problem, they are often more willing to talk about how to fix it.

Where to be careful

Humor works only when everyone is included. Jokes about a person's appearance, background, beliefs or abilities can hurt and can break workplace rules. A good test is whether the person being joked about would laugh too. If you are unsure, leave it out.

Humor and respect for work

Joking about work does not mean taking it lightly. The best teams laugh and still deliver. A shared sense of humor often goes along with trust, and trust helps people admit mistakes early, which makes the whole team better.

How to encourage it

Leaders can set the tone by laughing at themselves, not at others. Teams can keep a shared channel for harmless jokes, memes about common tasks and celebrations of small wins. Keep it optional, and respect people who prefer a quieter style.

A workplace with no laughter is rarely a better workplace. A little well-placed humor can turn a group of people who happen to share a building into a team.
