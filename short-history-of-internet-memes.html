A Short History of Internet Memes

Published November 2026 · 2 min read

From early email jokes to today's image macros and short videos.

The word 'meme' was introduced by the biologist Richard Dawkins in 1976 to describe an idea that spreads from person to person, much like a gene. Long before the internet, jokes, slogans and songs already travelled this way. The internet simply made the spread much faster.

In the 1990s, funny pictures and chain emails moved around inboxes, and early forums gave people places to share edited images. In the 2000s, image macros appeared: a picture with a short caption on top and bottom. These were easy to make and easy to remix, which is why they spread so widely.

Social networks in the 2010s changed how memes worked. Pages dedicated to humor could reach hundreds of thousands of followers, and a good joke could circle the world in a day. Formats became shared templates that thousands of people reused with their own text.

Today memes are also short videos, sounds and screenshots of everyday conversations. The format keeps changing, but the core idea stays the same: a small, quickly understood piece of humor that people adapt and pass on.

For anyone making memes, the history carries a lesson. The formats that last are simple enough for anyone to copy and flexible enough to fit many situations. Originality matters too. Readers enjoy a fresh take on a familiar feeling more than a copy of a joke they have already seen.

Why formats keep changing

Platforms shape what spreads. Forums favored images with text, social networks favored quick reactions, and short video apps favored sounds and clips. When people move to a new place, their humor adapts to the tools there.

What stays the same

Underneath the changing formats, memes still do the same jobs: they make people laugh, create a feeling of belonging and let strangers share an inside joke. A joke from twenty years ago and one from this week often use the same trick of surprise and recognition.

A tip for creators

Learn from how formats evolve, but make your own version rather than copying an existing image. Fresh wording on a familiar feeling will always feel more alive than a copy of last year's joke.
