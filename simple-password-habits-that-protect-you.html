Simple Password Habits That Protect You

Published November 2026 · 2 min read

Practical steps to make your accounts harder to break into.

Almost everything we do online depends on passwords, and weak ones are among the most common reasons accounts are taken over. You do not need to become a security expert to improve. A few steady habits go a long way.

Use a different password for every account

If you reuse one password, a leak at one website exposes every account that shares it. Criminals take leaked passwords and try them elsewhere. A separate password for each account limits the damage to one place.

Make passwords long, not just complicated

Length matters more than clever symbols. A passphrase made of several unrelated words, such as four or five random words joined together, is long, easy to remember and hard to guess. Avoid names, birthdays, favorite teams and simple patterns like the keyboard row.

Use a password manager

Remembering dozens of long, unique passwords is not realistic. A password manager stores them securely and fills them in for you. You only have to remember one strong master passphrase. Choose a well-known manager from a reputable company and protect it with two-step verification.

Turn on two-step verification

Even a strong password can leak. A second check, such as a code from an authenticator app, stops most takeover attempts. Turn it on for email, banking and social media first.

Be careful where you type your password

Check the web address before signing in, and be wary of links in emails or messages that ask you to log in. Do not enter passwords on shared or public computers, and sign out when you finish.

Do not share passwords in messages

Sending a password in a chat or email leaves a copy in places that may not be secure. If you must share access, use a feature built for it, such as a manager's sharing option, and change the password afterward.

Check for known leaks

Services exist that tell you whether an email address has appeared in a known data breach. If yours has, change the password for that site and for any other site where you used it.

Update the important ones first

If changing everything feels like too much, start with your main email account and your banking, because those open the door to everything else. Then work through the rest over a few weeks.

Good password habits are not about perfection. They are about making your accounts a harder target than the next person's, and a few of these steps are enough to do that.
